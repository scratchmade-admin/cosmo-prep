2) Quick study guide

A. Infection control, sanitation, and safety

Know the difference between:
- Cleaning: removes visible dirt and debris
- Disinfection: kills most harmful microorganisms on nonporous tools and surfaces
- Sterilization: destroys all microbial life, including spores

Important basics:
- Treat all blood and body fluids as potentially infectious
- Wash hands before and after services as needed
- Wear gloves when there is possible exposure to chemicals, blood, or body fluids
- Nonporous items like metal, glass, and hard plastic can usually be cleaned and disinfected
- Porous items usually cannot be properly disinfected and are often single-use

Common single-use items include:
- Cotton
- Tissues
- Orangewood sticks
- Neck strips
- Pumice bars
- Disposable buffers and files when required
- Wax sticks that touched skin

If there is blood exposure:
- Stop the service
- Put on gloves
- Clean the injured area
- Follow proper blood-exposure and disinfection procedure
- Dispose of contaminated materials correctly
- Wash hands

B. California laws and professional responsibility

Be ready for questions about:
- When not to perform a service
- Safe salon practices
- Infection-control rules
- License display requirements
- Scope of practice
- Consumer protection and professional conduct

Refuse or postpone service if:
- The client has a contagious condition
- There are open sores in the area to be serviced
- The service would be unsafe
- There is a contraindication

Stay in scope. Do not diagnose medical conditions. If something looks abnormal, refer the client to a physician.

C. Hair and scalp care

Hair structure:
- Cuticle: outer layer
- Cortex: middle layer, gives strength, elasticity, and color
- Medulla: innermost layer, not always present

Hair growth cycle:
- Anagen: growth
- Catagen: transition
- Telogen: resting

pH basics:
- 7 is neutral
- Below 7 is acidic
- Above 7 is alkaline

Alkaline products soften and swell the hair. More acidic products help smooth the cuticle.

D. Shampooing and conditioning

Know:
- Proper draping
- Comfortable water temperature
- When shampooing is not appropriate
- Conditioner purpose
- Scalp massage basics

Use the pads of the fingers, not fingernails.

E. Haircutting

Important terms:
- Elevation: angle at which hair is held while cutting
- Overdirection: combing hair away from its natural fall before cutting
- Guideline: section that shows the length to cut
- Tension: amount of pressure used when holding hair

Angles to remember:
- 0 degrees: blunt cut with more weight
- 45 degrees: graduated effect
- 90 degrees: uniform layers
- 180 degrees: long layers

Cross-checking helps confirm balance and accuracy.

F. Hairstyling and thermal styling

Know safe use of:
- Blow dryers
- Curling irons
- Flat irons
- Rollers
- Pin curls

Safety basics:
- Test tool temperature
- Avoid burning the scalp
- Never use damaged electrical cords
- Keep electrical tools away from water

G. Chemical waving and relaxing

Permanent waving:
- Hair is softened chemically
- It takes a new shape around the rod
- Neutralizer rebuilds the bonds in the new shape

Relaxers:
- Reduce the natural curl pattern
- Must not be applied to irritated or damaged scalp
- Overlapping on previously relaxed hair can cause breakage
- Always follow manufacturer directions carefully

H. Haircolor and lightening

Know the general differences:
- Temporary color: coats the hair and shampoos out
- Semi-permanent color: mainly deposits color
- Demi-permanent color: deposit-only with developer
- Permanent color: lifts and deposits

Other must-knows:
- Strand test helps predict processing result
- Patch test checks for allergic sensitivity as directed by manufacturer instructions
- Lighteners remove pigment and can damage hair if overprocessed

Basic color theory:
- Primary colors: red, yellow, blue
- Complementary colors neutralize each other
- Yellow is neutralized by violet
- Red is neutralized by green
- Blue is neutralized by orange

I. Skin care basics

Know:
- Basic skin structure
- Facial safety
- When a service should not be performed

Do not perform service if skin is broken, infected, contagious, or highly irritated.

J. Nail care basics

Know:
- Nail plate
- Nail bed
- Cuticle
- Matrix
- Lunula

Follow sanitation rules. Do not cut living skin aggressively.

K. Basic anatomy and chemistry

Review:
- Bones, muscles, nerves, skin, hair, nails
- Solutions, suspensions, emulsions
- Acids vs alkalis
- Oxidation-reduction basics
- Why manufacturer instructions matter

6) Explanations for the highest-value question types

Sanitation questions:
If you get stuck, choose the answer that protects the client, prevents contamination, and follows proper infection-control procedure.

Chemical questions:
When in doubt, look for the answer that includes proper analysis, scalp safety, manufacturer instructions, and no overlapping or guesswork.

Law and safety questions:
The best answer is usually the safest and most professional one. Refuse unsafe services, stay within scope, and do not diagnose medical conditions.

9) Test-taking strategy

- Read every question slowly
- Watch for words like first, best, most, except, and not
- Eliminate obviously unsafe answers first
- If two answers seem possible, choose the one that is more sanitary, more cautious, and more professional
- Do not change answers just because nerves get loud
