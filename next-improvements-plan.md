# Next Improvements Plan

Goal: improve the product tomorrow in ways that make it more useful without turning it into a bloated course platform.

## Prioritized next 10 improvements

### 1. Add a post-quiz "What Melissa should study next" block
**Priority:** Highest  
**Helps:** Melissa most

Right now the quiz gives topic breakdowns, but the next step is still a little implied. Add a short ranked recommendation block after results:
- Review these 2 weakest topics first
- Estimated review order
- Direct links to the matching study-guide sections

Why this matters: it removes one more thinking step. Melissa should not have to interpret the data herself when she is already stressed.

Lean version:
- No accounts
- No saved history
- Just a simple results-to-review recommendation on the same page

---

### 2. Add anchor links from quiz weak areas straight into the study guide
**Priority:** High  
**Helps:** Melissa most, classmates adoption

If the result says "Chemical Services needs work," that card should link directly to Module 6. Same for sanitation, law and safety, and the rest.

Why this matters: the app already has the right content. It just needs tighter movement between diagnosis and review.

Lean version:
- Add section IDs in the study guide
- Add direct "Review this topic" links in the result cards

---

### 3. Turn the home page CTA into a clearer funnel
**Priority:** High  
**Helps:** classmates adoption

The home page is solid, but it can do a better job converting curious classmates into quiz starters. Add one compact section above the final CTA that shows:
- what happens in 10 minutes
- what they get after finishing
- why this is better than random quiz sets

Why this matters: classmates need a simpler reason to try it now, not just a general description.

Lean version:
- One short section only
- No redesign needed
- Keep the whole message focused on “take quiz, find weak spots, review smarter”

---

### 4. Add a tiny confidence marker on each quiz question
**Priority:** High  
**Helps:** Melissa most

After answering, let the student mark: `Sure`, `Unsure`, or `Guess`. This is useful because many misses come from false confidence, and many lucky correct answers still reveal weak knowledge.

Why this matters: Melissa can study based on shaky understanding, not just wrong answers.

Lean version:
- No analytics dashboard
- Just use confidence tags in the result summary: “You guessed correctly on 4 questions. Review those too.”

---

### 5. Add a timed mode toggle for the same 20-question quiz
**Priority:** Medium-high  
**Helps:** Melissa most, future monetization

Timed mode is already on the launch plan and it is one of the few feature adds that increases real exam readiness without much bloat.

Why this matters: some students know the content but get sloppy under time pressure.

Lean version:
- Optional toggle at the top: Practice mode or Timed mode
- Simple countdown
- Reuse the same question set and results page

---

### 6. Expand the free quiz from one mixed set into 3 focused mini-quizzes
**Priority:** Medium-high  
**Helps:** classmates adoption, future monetization

Keep the current mixed quiz, but add a few short focused quizzes such as:
- Sanitation and law
- Chemical services
- Mixed final check

Why this matters: one quiz proves the concept. A few focused quizzes make the app feel more complete and more obviously useful.

Lean version:
- 10 questions each
- Reuse the current page structure
- No giant question bank yet

---

### 7. Add a one-page "3-day cram plan" page instead of burying it as a promise
**Priority:** Medium  
**Helps:** Melissa most, classmates adoption

The home page says students get 3-day and 7-day cram plans, but the current pages do not visibly deliver that yet. Ship the 3-day version first.

Why this matters: it makes the product feel concrete and test-ready. It also gives stressed students a starting point immediately.

Lean version:
- One standalone page
- Day-by-day review order
- Link to modules and quiz sections
- No downloadable PDF yet

---

### 8. Add a simple email capture before showing the "fuller practice" results or bonus study plan
**Priority:** Medium  
**Helps:** future monetization, classmates adoption

If Antoine wants validation and future conversion data, this is the cleanest next step. Offer something small in exchange:
- results emailed
- 3-day cram plan
- “next 50 questions” waitlist

Why this matters: it starts measuring real demand instead of guessing.

Lean version:
- One field only: email
- Optional or soft gate, not a hard wall at first
- Keep it low-friction

---

### 9. Add a lightweight pricing/preorder page for the full version
**Priority:** Medium  
**Helps:** future monetization

Do not build accounts and checkout first. Build the page that tests whether people want more.

Suggested offer framing:
- Free: study guide + sample quizzes
- Paid: full question bank, mock exam, weak-area review pack, timed mode

Why this matters: it validates the monetization angle before engineering the full paid product.

Lean version:
- One pricing page
- Stripe link or waitlist CTA
- No full member system yet

---

### 10. Add a short trust section with proof signals and disclaimers handled cleanly
**Priority:** Medium  
**Helps:** classmates adoption, future monetization

For classmates and referrals, credibility matters. Add a small section that explains:
- California-focused written exam prep
- independent resource, not official board material
- practical explanations, not fluff
- built for busy students and retakers

Why this matters: people hesitate on study products that feel vague or scammy. A little trust framing helps conversion.

Lean version:
- Short copy block on home page and pricing page
- No testimonials needed yet if none exist

## Do not do yet

These are tempting, but they are too early for tomorrow:

- **User accounts**: useful later, but unnecessary before stronger content and demand signals
- **Full progress tracking dashboard**: sounds impressive, not essential yet
- **Mobile app**: absolutely not yet
- **Spanish translation**: good expansion path, but only after the English flow proves itself
- **More license categories** like esthetician, barber, or nail tech: stay focused on one audience first
- **Flashcards**: not bad, just not the highest leverage next move
- **Big visual redesign**: current product strength is clarity, not cosmetics
- **Huge question-bank buildout before validation**: add enough to deepen value, not enough to disappear into content production

## Short summary

Best next move: tighten the loop between quiz results and exactly what to study next. After that, improve conversion with a clearer home-page funnel, a few more targeted quizzes, and a light monetization test before building heavier product infrastructure.
